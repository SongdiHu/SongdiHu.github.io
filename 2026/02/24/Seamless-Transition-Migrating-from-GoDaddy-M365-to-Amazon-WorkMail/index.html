Seamless Transition: Migrating from GoDaddy (M365) to Amazon WorkMail

Transitioning an organization’s email infrastructure is often met with trepidation. This week, I successfully managed the migration of a company’s email system from GoDaddy-hosted Microsoft 365 to Amazon WorkMail.

Here is a summary of the experience, the technical hurdles encountered, and the best practices for a zero-downtime cutover.

Why Amazon WorkMail?

While Microsoft 365 is a robust platform, Amazon WorkMail offers a simplified, secure, and cost-effective alternative that integrates natively with the AWS ecosystem. For organizations already leveraging AWS for hosting (Route 53, EC2, etc.), moving email to WorkMail reduces vendor sprawl and consolidates billing.

The Migration Strategy: Audriga

To handle the heavy lifting, we used audriga, the AWS-partnered migration service. It’s a web-based tool that handles the complex task of copying emails, calendars, and contacts between cloud providers.

The 4-Step Workflow:

  1. Provisioning: Creating the organization and user mailboxes in the Amazon WorkMail Console.
  2. Authentication: Connecting the “Source” (GoDaddy) and “Destination” (AWS).
  3. Full Sync: Copying historical data (the “long haul”).
  4. MX Cutover & Delta Sync: Switching the DNS and running a final pass to catch “gap” emails.

Technical Hurdles & Practical Solutions

1. The “Credential Trap” (MFA & SSO)

The biggest challenge when migrating from GoDaddy is their proprietary SSO/MFA layer. Standard user credentials often fail when used by third-party migration tools.

  • The Fix: Use Admin Impersonation. By authorizing a single Global Admin account, you gain access to all mailboxes without needing individual user passwords.

2. Connection Latency & Timeouts

During the authentication phase, we experienced intermittent timeouts and slow API responses.

  • The Fix: Modern Auth (OAuth2) can be sensitive to browser cache and peak-hour traffic. Switching to Chrome Incognito mode and performing the handshake during off-peak windows significantly improved stability.

3. Monitoring the “Black Box”

If you close your browser tab or miss the notification emails, it can feel like you’ve lost control of the migration.

  • The Fix: Always bookmark your unique audriga status link. If lost, a quick reach-out to their support with your Order ID can restore access.

The Secret Sauce: The “Delta Migration”

One of the most common fears in email migration is data loss during DNS propagation (the time it takes for the world to realize your email has moved).

We solved this by performing a Delta Migration. After switching the MX records in Route 53, we triggered one final sync. This captured the few emails that were still being delivered to the old GoDaddy servers during the transition period, ensuring a 100% data integrity rate.

Key Takeaways for Engineers

  • Preparation is King: Ensure your destination mailboxes are active and have sufficient storage before starting.
  • Admin Access is Mandatory: Don’t try to migrate users one by one; get Global Admin rights from the start.
  • Don’t Rush the DNS: Only flip the MX records once the initial sync is confirmed “Finished” in the migration dashboard.

Conclusion

Moving to Amazon WorkMail doesn’t have to be a headache. With the right tools and a structured approach—specifically utilizing Admin Impersonation and Delta Syncs—you can achieve a migration that is invisible to the end-users but highly beneficial for the organization’s bottom line.

Have questions about AWS WorkMail or migration tools? Feel free to reach out!
